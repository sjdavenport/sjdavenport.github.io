Latvia: a democracy that held

On V-Dem's Electoral Democracy Index, Latvia scores 0.83 in 2025 — essentially unchanged from 0.82 two decades ago, and above its own interwar peak. It is classified a liberal democracy, ranks 3rd of 30 in Eastern Europe & Central Asia, and its courts are rated more independent than 99.9% of all country-years V-Dem has ever recorded. The interesting story here is not decline but durability.

What the 2025 score is made of

Polyarchy aggregates five components. Unlike a backsliding case, Latvia scores high on all five — the two that autocracies typically hollow out first, clean elections and free expression, are its strongest sub-scores at 0.92 and 0.93.

Components over time, 1991–2025

The same five components across the restored republic. The build-out is visible in the 1990s: clean elections climb from 0.69 to 0.92 and free association from 0.63 to 0.89, while suffrage and elected officials are maxed from the start. Since about 2000 every line is flat — no component trends down, and the composite's small wobbles are measurement noise, not a directional slide.

Where it stands on specific indicators, 2015 → 2025

Each expert-coded indicator is placed as a percentile against all 19,857 country-years in the dataset (1900–2025). Position 0 = worse than everything ever recorded; 100 = better than everything. Grey dot = 2015, coloured dot = 2025. Latvia sits in the top decile on nearly everything; high court independence is at the 99.9th percentile. The mild retreats are in the media indicators — still 86th–88th percentile, but modestly worse than 2015.

Separately, political corruption (v2x_corr, where higher = worse) fell from 0.082 to 0.070 — the 13th percentile for corruption, i.e. cleaner than 87% of country-years.

Latvia vs. its neighbours, 1991–2025

Latvia and Estonia both start low in 1991 and converge on the Nordic/Baltic band within a decade; Finland is flat and high throughout. The two large movements belong to others — Estonia's early-90s dip (citizenship rules left much of the Russophone population without the vote) and Poland's 2015–2023 decline and recovery under and after PiS. Latvia shows neither.

How to read these numbers

Every score is expert-coded and modelled, not an official statistic: V-Dem asks multiple in-country experts to rate each indicator, then combines their answers with a Bayesian measurement model that also produces the uncertainty band shown above. The 0–1 indices are interval measures of "how far toward the democratic ideal," not percentages. The granular indicators use a different, roughly −3.5–+3.5 expert scale. Note that Latvia's regime classification flips between electoral and liberal democracy several times (2009, 2013, 2014, 2016, 2017) — those flips sit within the model's uncertainty and should not be read as real regime change. 2025 is the newest and least-settled year in v16 (released early 2026). Full definitions are in each dataset folder's codebook.pdf.
