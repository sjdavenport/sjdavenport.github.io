Serbia: two decades of democratic backsliding

On V-Dem's Electoral Democracy Index, Serbia has fallen from 0.65 at its 2003 peak to 0.30 in 2025 — a drop of 0.35 points. In 2013 it was reclassified from a liberal democracy to an electoral autocracy, where it remains. It now ranks 23rd of 30 in Eastern Europe & Central Asia.

What's driving it: specific indicators, 2015 → 2025

The underlying expert-coded indicators are on V-Dem's own scale, which is hard to read in the abstract — so each is placed as a percentile against all 19,857 country-years in the dataset (1900–2025). Position 0 = worse than everything ever recorded; 100 = better than everything. Grey dot = 2015, coloured dot = 2025. The starkest is election irregularities, now at the 5th percentile — worse than 95% of all country-years on record.

Separately, political corruption (v2x_corr, where higher = worse) rose from 0.65 to 0.79 — putting Serbia in the 86th percentile for corruption, more corrupt than 86% of country-years.

How to read these numbers

Every score is expert-coded and modelled, not an official statistic: V-Dem asks multiple in-country experts to rate each indicator, then combines their answers with a Bayesian measurement model that also produces the uncertainty band shown above. The 0–1 indices are interval measures of "how far toward the democratic ideal," not percentages. The granular indicators use a different, roughly −3.5–+3.5 expert scale. 2025 is the newest and least-settled year in v16 (released early 2026), so treat the most recent drops as provisional. Full definitions are in each dataset folder's codebook.pdf.
